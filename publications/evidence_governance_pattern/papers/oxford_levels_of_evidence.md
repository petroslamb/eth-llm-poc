# OCEBM Levels of Evidence

## Introduction

* [Download the Levels of Evidence document](https://www.cebm.ox.ac.uk/files/levels-of-evidence/cebm-levels-of-evidence-2-1.pdf)(v2.1, PDF)

NB: the table is intended to be used alongside the [Introductory Document](https://www.cebm.ox.ac.uk/files/levels-of-evidence/cebm-levels-of-evidence-introduction-2-1.pdf)and [Background Document.](https://www.cebm.ox.ac.uk/files/levels-of-evidence/cebm-levels-of-evidence-background-document-2-1.pdf) Please **do not** read the table separately but rather read the Introductory Document and Table together.

Hierarchies of evidence have been somewhat inflexibly used, and criticised, for some decades. The CEBM “levels of evidence” were first produced in 1998 for *Evidence-Based On Call* to make the process of finding appropriate evidence feasible and its results explicit. We have revised the “levels” in light of new concepts and data, and we would like to hear your [feedback](https://www.cebm.ox.ac.uk/about-us/contact-us "Contact").

There are two different ways to interpret Level 1 evidence for treatment benefits as it is currently stated. The intended interpretation is: “either N-of-1 randomized trials or systematic reviews of randomized trials”. The wrong interpretation is: “either systematic reviews of randomized trials or systematic reviews of n-of-1 trials”.
